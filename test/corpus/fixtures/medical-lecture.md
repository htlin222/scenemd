# Acute Myeloid Leukemia

AML is a clonal hematopoietic malignancy defined by proliferation of myeloid blasts and impaired differentiation.

## Diagnosis

Diagnosis integrates morphology, immunophenotyping, cytogenetics, and molecular genetics.

- Morphology: at least 20% blasts in marrow or blood
- Flow cytometry: myeloid antigen expression
- Cytogenetics: recurrent translocations define entities
- Molecular testing: NPM1, FLT3, CEBPA, TP53

### Risk stratification

| Risk | Genetics | Approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Favorable | t(8;21), inv(16), NPM1mut without FLT3-ITD | Consolidation chemotherapy |
| Intermediate | Normal karyotype, FLT3-ITD low | Transplant per response |
| Adverse | Complex karyotype, TP53 | Transplant when feasible |

## Treatment

Treatment depends on age, fitness, disease biology, and targetable mutations.

- Intensive induction: cytarabine plus anthracycline
- Hypomethylating agent plus venetoclax for unfit patients
- FLT3 inhibitors added for FLT3-mutated disease
- CNS prophylaxis in selected presentations

### Response assessment

Marrow at recovery defines remission. Measurable residual disease refines it.

$$
\text{MRD-negative CR} \Rightarrow \text{superior OS and RFS}
$$

## Prognosis

> Fitness for intensive therapy changes the decision tree more than any single mutation.

Long-term survival tracks genetics, age, and transplant access.
